# BotBound Brand System

Production identity approved 2026-08-20.

Current public positioning reviewed October 23, 2026. Existing campaign images, videos and historical proof retain their original copy until separately refreshed; this source update does not recertify those assets.

## The Idea

BotBound gives people and small businesses voice Bots with a computer, phone number, and email address, available through the workspace and selected plan. The launch story starts with answering calls, handling useful requests, sending approved email, and supervised Computer work. The identity makes the phone capability literal: a calm, friendly robot visibly grips a classic phone receiver to its ear. The hand is essential. It turns a generic support Bot silhouette into a specific, ownable operator.

The brand line is:

> Your Next Hire Is a Bot.

The current homepage display is:

> VOICE BOTS.
> REAL WORK.

Its supporting line is: “YOUR BOT GETS THEIR OWN PHONE NUMBER, EMAIL ADDRESS, AND COMPUTER”. Follow it with “Choose the channels you need.” The hero introduces the product family; individual prices and channel entitlements belong in the plan cards. Personal connects an existing inbox rather than providing a managed mailbox.

The general product category is **Voice Bots**. The launch campaign line is **Your Next Hire Is a Bot.** The prepared **Give Your Bot a Goal.** family remains a creative variant and uses the approved operator and the existing Stay in the Work photograph. Historical campaigns remain dated creative variants; “hire” describes software, not an employment service. See [the goal campaign copy kit](launch-2026-10-goal/COPY-KIT.md).

SMS and outbound calling are included in the product. Do not describe them as coming soon, pending, or missing from the plan. Recipient permission still applies. Reviewed personal and specific business service calls remain subject to the current eligibility controls. Do not promise that personal branding removes those controls. The [founder launch runbook](../docs/FOUNDER_LAUNCH_COMMAND_CENTER.md) owns the current scope and proof requirements.

The homepage describes the product family. A dedicated number requires an eligible Calls plan; a managed inbox requires an eligible Email plan. Computer work starts as its own browser task. Do not imply that one task automatically receives the Bot's call or email history.

## Primary Identity

- Use the approved white and platinum robot with the violet receiver.
- Keep the receiver on the robot's left side, pressed to the ear, with the white hand visibly wrapped around it.
- Keep both B glyphs in `BotBound` complete. Never notch, stencil, split, crop, or turn either B into a pictogram.
- Use title case `BotBound` in product and brand lockups. `BOTBOUND` is reserved for short campaign labels, URLs, and small mono utility copy.
- Set the wordmark in Inter Tight ExtraBold, weight 800, with optical tracking near `-0.047em`.
- Set `Bot` in platinum or obsidian and `Bound` in Aurora violet. Do not gradient-fill the wordmark.

## Clear Space and Minimum Size

- Clear space around the app icon: at least one antenna-ball diameter on every side.
- Clear space around the horizontal lockup: at least the height of the lowercase `o` on every side.
- Minimum app icon: 24 px in UI, 32 px preferred, 48 px for marketing navigation.
- Minimum horizontal lockup: 144 px wide on screen.
- At tiny sizes, use the app icon, not the transparent operator.
- Do not place the white operator directly on white or uncontrolled photography. Use the full app icon or an obsidian field.

## Typography

- Display: Inter Tight, 700. Wordmark: Inter Tight, 800.
- Product body: Inter, 400 to 700.
- Operational labels and campaign eyebrows: IBM Plex Mono, 700, uppercase, wide tracking.
- Public hero messages use uppercase, short phrases, and balanced lines. The homepage has a larger two-line display; interior pages share a 32 to 60 px responsive tier with 1.04 line-height and restrained negative tracking. Avoid isolated final words and manual breaks that fail on smaller screens.
- Center public hero content unless the page deliberately uses a split editorial layout. Align its title, supporting copy, and actions to the same axis. Remove redundant decorative eyebrows and audience badges. Public display and section headings use uppercase; product names, legal prose, body copy, controls and customer text retain authored case.
- Prefer short, standalone bullet points for self-explanatory capabilities. Do not add a sentence that repeats the label. Keep supporting detail when it explains a meaningful limit, decision, or next step.

### Public Interface Rhythm

- Use one 1120 px content rail and responsive 20 to 32 px page gutters.
- Use 24, 32, 48, and 80 px spacing relationships. Sections size to their content and use the browser document for scrolling.
- Keep controls at least 44 px high; hero actions use 48 px. Show full action labels on small screens and let enlarged text reflow.
- Prefer quiet borders, limited card surfaces, and left-aligned detail inside cards. Violet signals the primary action or current state.
- The header answers two buying questions: Features and Pricing. Keep account entry plan-neutral; group secondary public routes in the footer.
- Avoid duplicate fixed conversion bars when the page already has clear actions. Preserve useful limits, delivery errors, pricing disclosures, and recovery paths.
- `src/styles/public-design-system.css` owns shared public composition. The landing page owns its larger display; the authenticated workspace keeps its own density.

## Photography and Image Direction

Show competent people in the middle of real work: hands occupied, attention committed, environment specific. Light scenes like prestige craft advertising, not generic software stock.

- Use deep practical shadows, warm work light, and restrained violet signal light.
- Preserve intentional negative space for campaign copy.
- The robot may enter the world as a believable premium character, but must retain the approved white/platinum shell, black visor, friendly violet eyes, antenna, visible gripping hand, and classic violet receiver. Use the dimensional polished operator for hero placements and the flat operator only when the layout needs graphic simplicity.
- Avoid humanoid faces, metallic menace, headset silhouettes, floating chat bubbles, neon overload, fake interface screens, and text generated inside source photography.

## Motion and Sound

The signature motion is the second ring: the first ring belongs to the missed call, and the second ring becomes the violet BotBound signal. Signal rings should expand from the receiver with soft decay, never pulse continuously like an alarm.

- Use confident 10 to 18 frame eases and restrained spring response.
- Let practical sound lead before music.
- Use the two-note transfer chime as the sonic seed: D5 to A5, clean and warm.
- The phone answer moment should reduce the score and let human voice carry the proof.

## Product Application

- Public navigation uses the complete wordmark without a repeated robot. The approved operator anchors the homepage.
- Hero lockups use the display size with an 84 px mark and 38.7 px wordmark.
- Compact product chrome uses a 32 px app icon and a complete 800-weight wordmark.
- All web, Stripe, browser extension, iOS, and Android outputs derive from the one app-icon master.
- Use raster masters in production. SVG files in legacy paths are compatibility wrappers only, not alternate drawings.

## Do Not

- Do not break either B.
- Do not redraw the robot.
- Do not remove the hand.
- Do not add a second ear pad or make the receiver read as a headset.
- Do not gradient-fill `BotBound`.
- Do not put the transparent operator on a light field without contrast support.
- Do not recolor the receiver away from the violet family.
- Do not pair the mark with the retired Wireline W.
